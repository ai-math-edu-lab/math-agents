# B(2,5) trivial words — 2.5-reduced, unique (820)

> [!info] Plain English
> 820 words in a, b and their inverses, each **proved to equal the identity in the free Burnside group B(2,5)**. None can be shortened by the standard 2.5-power rewriting, and no two are the same word up to rotation, inversion, or swapping/inverting the letters. Use them as hard target words for B(2,5) challenge generation and benchmarking. The proof is a Knuth–Bendix rewriting run that takes seconds to reproduce; no per-word derivation is stored.

**Status: superseded 2026-10-14 by [[ds-b25-trivial-2-5reduced-aut8-20261014]]** (T-012): the claim "fixed point of the reducer" is false for lines 14 and 15 as stored. The file and its sha256 are unchanged. Originally validated by the owner (asetlearning), 2026-10-07.

## What it is
- **Data file:** one cyclic word per line, lengths 32–123 (mean 71.2, median 67), sorted by length and then lexicographically. Each line is the canonical representative of its class. Example (line 1): `AABAABabaabAAbaabaBAABAABabaabaB`.
- **Sources file** (`.sources.csv`): one row per word, in the same order: `index, word, length, n_cyclic_classes, n_source_words, sources, decomposable_by`.
  - `sources` lists every parent word that collapsed into this class. `rpo-NNNN` / `slx-NNNN` is line NNNN (1-based) of the rpo / shortlex parent file.
  - `decomposable_by` is non-empty for the 9 words that contain another dataset word as a block (see Caveats).
- **Origin of classes:** 510 come only from rpo, 267 only from shortlex, 43 from both.

## Origin
- **Where the words came from (owner).** The words come from rewrite rules generated during the Knuth–Bendix runs on B(2,5) in March 2026.
- **The runs.**
  - Program: kbmag `kbprog`, on the presentation `algo-mixer:experiments/_inputs/kbmag/b25_full` (sha256 `b1d511fe…ba32ee`).
  - Orderings: recursive (RPO, `-rec`) and shortlex, run side by side with rule injection. See [[kbmag-data]] and [[kbmag-overlap-scoring-b25-2026-05-22]].
  - Rule banks: algo-mixer `runs/b25/20260320_*`.
- **The parent files.**
  - The RPO rules gave `rpo_trivial_no_powers_20260321.txt` (917 words).
  - The shortlex rules gave `shortlex_trivial_no_powers_20260321.txt` (2378 words).
  - Both were saved 2026-03-21. They are kept untracked in the fork's `data/` and are not committed (owner decision), so the sha256 values in `derived_from` are their only durable identity.
- **Not recorded:** how a rule became a word (e.g. lhs·rhs⁻¹), and what exactly the "no powers" filter removed. Neither matters for validity: every word was re-verified from scratch (below).

## Derivation
1. **Parents:** the rpo (917) and shortlex (2378) sets. All 3295 words are freely and cyclically reduced.
2. **2.5-reduction:** each word was reduced with `greedy_reduce_cyclic_exhaustive2_5` (tcgraph, all cyclic shifts, repeated until no change).
   - 180 words got shorter, and 152 of them reduced to the empty word.
   - The 152 were dropped: they are trivially trivial, so useless as challenges.
   - The other 28 were kept in their reduced form. Each is a conjugate of the original, so triviality is preserved.
3. **Cyclic dedup:** each word was replaced by the shortlex-least rotation of itself or its inverse. This leaves 1094 classes.
4. **Letter automorphisms:** the 8 length-preserving automorphisms of F(a,b) (swap a↔b, invert a, invert b, and their combinations) map trivial words to trivial words. Classes were merged under them, and each class keeps the shortlex-least canonical form among the members present. This leaves **820** words.

### Triviality in B(2,5): Knuth–Bendix reduction
- **Why it is a proof.**
  - All 815 equations of `b25_full` are powers v⁵ᵏ of words with |v| ≤ 7 (checked by script), so each holds in B(2,5).
  - Every rule kbprog derives is a consequence of these equations and free cancellation, so it is an equality in B(2,5) even though the system is not confluent.
  - A word that rewrites to the empty word is therefore trivial in B(2,5).
  - The file's header comment mentions RB(2,5), but its equations are powers only.
- **Procedure.**
  1. Copy `b25_full` twice: `rec` (ordering `recursive`, as in the file) and `slx` (ordering changed to `shortlex`).
  2. Run `kbprog -me 100000 -silent <name>`. This gives RPO 97,484 rules in 10 s and shortlex 99,948 rules in 2 s, both non-confluent.
  3. Add `maxeqns := 2000000` to `<name>.kbprog` so `wordreduce` loads every rule.
  4. Run `wordreduce -kbprog <name>` on every word (`kb_reduce_words.py`).
- **Results.**

  | Words | RPO system | shortlex system |
  |---|---|---|
  | rpo parents (917) | 917 | 917 |
  | shortlex parents (2378) | 2378 | 2378 |
  | this dataset (820) | 819 (misses line 431) | 819 (misses line 41) |
  | rotations + inverse rotations of line 431 (136) | 114 | 136 |
  | negative controls (825) | 0 | 0 |

  Every one of the 820 words reduces directly to the identity under at least one system, and 818 under both. The negative controls are the 820 words with one random letter inverted, plus the short words `a`, `ab`, `abAB`, `aabAB` and `ababABAB`. None reduces, so the systems are not degenerate.
- **How many rules are needed.** Smaller shortlex systems already prove most of the words:

  | Rule cap (`-me`) | Words proved (of 820) |
  |---|---|
  | 2,000 | 790 |
  | 5,000 | 813 |
  | 10,000 | 815 |
  | 20,000 | 816 |
  | 50,000 | 819 |

- **Trust.** The proof relies on kbmag's rewriting being correct; two independent orderings agree. No per-word factor-word certificate is stored. Re-running the procedure reproduces the result in under a minute.
- **Where it ran:** VM `ubuntu-gnu-linux-24-04-3`, 2026-10-07. Run dir: `~/Research/challenge-gen/runs/kb-verify-20261007/` (local), with `provenance.json`, per-word CSVs `rec100k.csv` / `slx100k.csv` and `sizes/`.

### Triviality in B₀(2,5): GAP
- **Group.** `PqEpimorphism(F : Prime:=5, Exponent:=5, ClassBound:=12)`. The script checks that the result has size 5³⁴ and class 12.
- **Controls.** a⁵ and (ab)⁵ come out trivial; a, a⁴ and [a,b] do not.
- **What was checked.** All 3295 parent words, in original and 2.5-reduced form (6590 evaluations, 29 s), were trivial. The 820 strings are rotations, inverses and automorphic images of checked words.
- **Origin of the script.** `b0_challenge_triviality.g`, adapted from algo-mixer `runs/b25/b0-challenge-triviality/20260930T200842Z` (see [[2026-09-30-b25-b0-challenge-triviality]]).
- **Run dir:** `~/Research/challenge-gen/runs/trivial-no-powers-verify-20261007/`.

### Code versions
- **2.5-reduction:** `tcgraph_ext` built in the VM from b25_pyproject_agentic@7e50fea against tcgraph_agentic@a0d5e92. b25 pins tcgraph c093277, which does not compile `bindings_pattern_boost.cpp`. `greedy_reduction.{h,cpp}` and `word.{h,cpp}` are identical in the two versions.
- **Scripts:** `check_words.py`, `combine.py`, `kb_reduce_words.py` and `b0_challenge_triviality.g`, with a README that gives the exact commands. They are in b25_pyproject_agentic, branch `tools/challenge-gen-datasets`, under `experiments/challenge_gen/` (not yet committed).

## Caveats
- **9 decomposable words are included.** Each contains another dataset word (up to rotation or inverse) as a block, so it is conjugate to a product of two shorter dataset words. Seven share the same 42-letter block. They are flagged in `decomposable_by`; filter them out if you need indecomposable words (811 remain).
- **Redundancy beyond the automorphisms is not removed.** Many words share long blocks, and no near-duplicate clustering was done.
- **Representatives are canonical forms, not the parent spellings.** Map back through the sources file.
